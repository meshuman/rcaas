RCAAS Technology documents temples, stupas, palaces and historic towns across Nepal, and turns those records into experiences that let people everywhere explore them.

2. Why now #why-now
H2: Documenting what cannot be replaced
Body: Nepal's temples, stupas, palaces and historic towns are living records of its history. Weather, earthquakes, development and time all change them, and the 2015 earthquake showed how quickly irreplaceable structures can be damaged or lost. When that happens, a photograph or an old drawing is rarely enough to understand what a place truly was, or to guide its restoration.
A measured 3D record keeps that knowledge. It can be studied from any angle, measured years later and compared over time. And when it is shared, it lets people who may never stand in a courtyard in Kathmandu experience it for themselves.

11. FAQ #faq
H2: Questions about heritage documentation
Q: Why document heritage in 3D? Photos and drawings show a place from fixed viewpoints. A 3D record can be measured, studied from any angle and compared over time. If a structure is damaged or lost, the record can guide its restoration.
Q: Does scanning damage monuments? No. Laser scanning and drone capture are completely contactless. We never touch the structure.
Q: Do you work on sacred or protected sites? Yes, with respect and with proper permission. We follow the rules of each site and work with the people who care for it.
Q: How accurate is a heritage record? [[TBI: accuracy by method]]. We agree the accuracy your conservation or research work needs at the start and tell you if it is achievable.
Q: Who owns the heritage data? [[TBI: ownership and access terms, e.g. client, heritage body, public access]]
Q: Can the public see the record? Yes, if the site's custodians agree. We can publish a photorealistic 3D experience separately from the technical data, so access to each can be managed.
