## 4. What we mean by a digital twin `#definition`
- **H2:** What we mean by "digital twin"
- **Body:** For us, a digital twin is a measured 3D record of a place as it is today. It is accurate enough to measure and design from, and it sits in real-world coordinates so it lines up with your maps and drawings. It can be updated with repeat captures to show change over time, and the same data can power a 3D tour or a film. ## 5. What we create `#capabilities`
- **H2:** What we create
- **Intro:** We choose the capture method for your site and purpose. Most projects combine ground and aerial capture, so interiors, roofs and surroundings join into one complete record.

### 5.1 3D laser scanning
- **H3:** 3D laser scanning
- **What it is:** A handheld laser scanner records every wall, beam and opening as our engineer walks through the space. A floor that takes days to measure by hand is captured in minutes.
- **You receive:** A measured 3D point cloud, plus drawings and models built from it.
- **Best for:** As-built surveys, renovation and fit-out, complex or irregular buildings, places that are slow or unsafe to measure by tape.
- **Made with:** Handheld SLAM laser scanners such as the XGRIDS Lixel Kitty K1.
- **Link:** Explore 3D laser scanning → `/services/digital-twins/3d-laser-scanning/`

### 5.2 Drone mapping and aerial survey
- **H3:** Drone mapping and aerial survey
- **What it is:** Planned survey flights that capture roofs, courtyards, terrain and whole sites from above, processed into true-scale maps and 3D models.
- **You receive:** Orthomosaic maps, surface and terrain models, 3D site models [[TBC: contours and base maps]].
- **Best for:** Site and land surveys, large heritage complexes, roof and facade records, planning, monitoring change.
- **Made with:** Survey drones such as the DJI Mavic 3E, with a mechanical-shutter camera for distortion-free images.
- **Link:** Explore drone mapping → `/services/digital-twins/drone-mapping/`

### 5.3 As-built drawings and CAD/BIM-ready data `#as-built`
- **H3:** As-built drawings and CAD/BIM-ready data
- **What it is:** Your scan turned into the drawings and files your design team works with.
- **You receive:** Measured floor plans, building sections, exterior elevations, point cloud reference clips for AutoCAD/Revit [[TBI: drawing scope, e.g. plans, sections, elevations]], and files for CAD, BIM and modelling software [[TBC]].
- **Best for:** Architects, interior designers, structural engineers and general contractors.

### 5.4 Survey, positioning and GIS `#survey-gis`
- **H3:** Survey, positioning and GIS
- **What it is:** The surveying that ties your scan to the real world: ground control, georeferencing, topographic survey, GIS mapping and land-use analysis.
- **You receive:** Georeferenced data placed accurately on the map, topographic site survey layers [[TBC]], and GIS geodatabase exports [[TBC]].
- **Best for:** Engineering projects, urban planning, municipal asset inventories, and infrastructure teams needing real-world coordinate alignment.
- **Made with:** Survey-grade GNSS receivers and ground control targets [[TBI: equipment]].

## 12. FAQ `#faq`
- **H2:** Questions about digital twins and survey

### How accurate is your data?
It depends on the method, the site and the ground control used [[TBI: accuracy achievable by method]]. We agree the accuracy your project needs during planning and tell you if it is achievable. Every project includes verification against independent check points.

### Can I use the data in CAD, BIM or GIS software?
Yes. We deliver data in open and industry-standard formats including E57 and LAS/LAZ for point clouds, DWG/DXF for vector CAD drawings, GeoTIFF for georeferenced aerial maps, and OBJ/FBX for 3D meshes.

### Can you produce floor plans and sections?
Yes. From the registered point cloud, our team drafts 2D architectural floor plans, reflected ceiling plans, building sections, and facade elevations in DWG and PDF formats.

### Is the data tied to real-world coordinates?
Yes, where your project needs it. We use survey-grade GNSS receivers and ground control points (GCPs) so your 3D dataset aligns with national coordinate systems, cadastral maps, and GIS databases.

### Does scanning damage buildings or monuments?
No. Laser scanning and drone aerial capture are completely contactless (non-invasive). Nothing touches the structure, making it ideal for fragile, medieval, or sacred cultural monuments.

### Can you support academic research or a PhD?
Yes. We can plan capture around your research question, advise on spatial data resolution and export point clouds or mesh models in formats needed for structural analysis, finite-element modeling, or archaeological documentation.

### Who owns the data?
You own all delivered files and survey datasets for your project. Full commercial usage rights are transferred upon delivery.
